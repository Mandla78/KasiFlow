===========================================================================
SUPPLIER 11 -- Q&A DEFENCE (what judges will ask; our answers)
Only claim what's built by then. Update after each phase.
===========================================================================

Q: Where do the suppliers come from? Are they real?
A: Suppliers connect their own systems to our integration API; for the
   demo they're fictional companies with a realistic catalogue of
   hundreds of products, imported through the SAME API and file import a
   real supplier's ERP would use. No supplier app to install.

Q: How does a supplier's ERP actually connect?
A: Three ways, because that's what SA wholesalers run: their system pushes
   to our REST API (per-supplier keys with scopes), a connector syncs both
   ways, or they upload CSV/JSON exports. Orders reach them by signed
   webhook or by polling. We can show a key being created, an import with
   a row-error report, a stock change appearing in the app.

Q: What stops a supplier (or a stolen key) touching another supplier's data?
A: Every integration query is scoped to the key's supplier; keys are
   hashed, scoped, expiring and instantly revocable; wrong scope -> 403,
   revoked -> 401; every use is audited.

Q: Can a trader change the price in the app and pay less?
A: No. The app sends products and quantities only; the server prices the
   order from the database and keeps a snapshot on each line.

Q: Do you hold the money?
A: No. In-app payments go through PayFast, and Split Payments pays the
   supplier's own PayFast account directly; cash is between the trader and
   the driver, and both confirm the amount. We never pay out.

Q: How do you know a payment is real?
A: Only PayFast's notification marks an order paid, after four checks:
   signature with our passphrase, it came from PayFast, the amount
   matches, and PayFast confirms it server-to-server. The app's return
   page can't mark anything paid.

Q: What about invoices and tax?
A: The invoice is the supplier's (they're the seller); we generate it
   from their details following SARS's rules (abridged up to R5,000, full
   above), with a gap-free number per supplier, and store its hash.

Q: Two traders order the last item at once?
A: Stock is held in the same database transaction with a conditional
   update; one order succeeds, the other is told it's out of stock.

Q: Why would suppliers integrate?
A: Orders from hundreds of spaza shops near them, paid in the app or in
   cash that's confirmed on both sides, with fewer disputes -- through the
   system they already run.

Q: POPIA?
A: Suppliers receive only what an order needs (business name, delivery
   address, a phone for the driver); a trader's customers and credit book
   never leave the trader's account.
