THE BUILDER'S REAL PROBLEMS (why jobs exists)
---------------------------------------------------------------------------
  1. Getting paid: clients pay late, pay less, or say "I already paid
     you for the roof". Cash, no slip. (Even formal construction: 89% of
     surveyed practitioners call late and non-payment chronic.)
  2. Scope creep: "while you're here, add a window" -- extra work agreed
     by mouth, never paid.
  3. Materials: the builder buys cement, bricks, sand with the client's
     deposit; runs out mid-stage; drives around for stock; can't show the
     client what the money went on.
  4. Knowing if a job made money: stage payments in, materials and
     casual workers out, nobody adds it up.
  5. Getting the next job: no portfolio, no proof of finished work,
     no record to show a bank or a bigger client.

WHAT STOPS THEM USING THE WHATSAPP SIGN-OFF (and questions they'll ask)
---------------------------------------------------------------------------
  THE CLIENT
    - "Is this a scam?" A link from a strange address that asks for money
      details. -> The message must look like the builder's own words,
      show the photo, and the page must ask for NOTHING personal. A real
      domain (not a tunnel address) before real clients.
    - Data and airtime: the page must be tiny (no big images: a
      compressed photo, < 100 KB total) and work on a slow phone.
    - Age and language: one screen, big buttons, plain words; later
      isiZulu / Sepedi / Xitsonga / Afrikaans.
    - "Why must I type what I paid?" -> Say it: "So you both have the same
      record. Nobody sees your number until you both answer."
  THE BUILDER
    - "It feels like I don't trust my client." -> Frame it as a receipt
      FOR THE CLIENT: "Here's the photo of the roof and your receipt."
    - "What if they never answer?" -> Reminders (the builder taps "remind",
      we prefill a polite nudge), and the stage stays "waiting" with a date.
    - "What if they answer a different amount?" -> Today: amounts don't
      match. Next: both see the two amounts side by side and can fix it
      once (one correction each, audited), or keep the disagreement on
      record.
    - "Is my client's number safe? Do you sell my data?" -> Plain answer
      in the app (no, and why).
    - "I'm on site, no signal." -> Photos wait on the phone and upload
      later; the server time is when it arrives, the phone's time is
      kept as "taken around".
    - "Do I pay for this?" -> Free for traders (suppliers pay).

SEND THE PHOTO ITSELF ON WHATSAPP (not only a link)  -- researched
---------------------------------------------------------------------------
  A client trusts a photo of their own roof far more than a link.
  - React Native's own Share and expo-sharing can't reliably send an
    image AND a text caption to WhatsApp on Android (the text is dropped
    or the image is).
  - react-native-share's shareSingle(WHATSAPP) sends the image file with
    the message (and the link) as its caption, straight into WhatsApp's
    contact picker. It is a NATIVE module: it needs its config plugin in
    app.json (android queries for com.whatsapp) and a NEW development
    build. Per our rules that's Mandla's call (QUESTION below).
  - Flow: download the stage photo to the cache (expo-file-system),
    shareSingle({ social: WHATSAPP, url: fileUri, type: 'image/jpeg',
    message }), where message already ends with the sign-off link.
  - Fallback when WhatsApp isn't installed: today's text-only link.

  QUESTION FOR MANDLA: add react-native-share (native, new dev build)?
    Recommendation: yes -- it's the difference between "a link" and
    "a photo of your roof, tap to confirm".
