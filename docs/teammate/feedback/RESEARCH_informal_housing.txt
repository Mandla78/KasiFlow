QUESTION WE HAD
  Builders are what no competitor serves (Vuleka, Shop2Shop and the
  rest are all about spaza shops). Is informal home building big enough,
  and does it fit what the app already does? With a close look at rural
  areas.


WHAT I FOUND

  1. Why people build for themselves
  - More than 2.3 million households need housing assistance, and 3.4
    million are on the housing needs database (Minister Simelane, Sep
    2026). The state delivered 23,027 of a targeted 37,779 houses in
    2025/26.
  - Most housing in Africa is built incrementally, by households
    themselves or by small builders they hire, paid from savings,
    family and personal loans (CAHF).
  - Building materials lead hardware sales because South Africa has a
    large owner-builder and contractor-led culture, in townships,
    peri-urban areas and rural provinces (Nexdigm).
  So: the state can't keep up, and families build in stages, with cash
  and a local builder.

  2. Rural areas (the strongest case)
  - Urban working- and middle-class South Africans are putting their
    life savings into new homes in the former homelands, not into city
    property (African Arguments; Daily Maverick).
  - Teachers, nurses and civil servants build without mortgages, in
    phases as money allows (The Brief, Aug 2026; its author says the
    scale needs more research).
  - Land on communal land comes from traditional authorities at little
    or no cost, but only with a Permission to Occupy, never a title
    deed. A PLAAS study in Limpopo villages (2021-22, 127 people):
    75.25% of those who got customary land in the past 10 years paid
    cash. No title deed means no mortgage, so the house goes up stage by
    stage, in cash, with no record.
  - In the former Transkei, wattle-and-daub rondavels are giving way to
    brick-and-mortar houses on family land (The Conversation). Census
    2022: 88.5% live in formal housing, and traditional and informal
    dwellings have fallen over 10 years. Every one of those brick
    houses is work for a local builder.
  - Chains like Cashbuild put stores in townships and rural areas and
    sell for cash to owner-builders and small contractors. Local
    hardware stores and brickyards are the suppliers who would pay us.

  3. Townships: the backroom boom (the same pattern, in the city)
  - About 1 million backroom rental units (2016 Human Settlements data).
    Market estimates range from R20bn to R50bn a year (Forbes Africa,
    quoting reports and GG Alcock's KasiNomics): say "billions a year".
  - Urban households in backyards rose from 8.9% (2011) to 13.4% (2016).
  - Small landlords are building, and funders back them: TUHF lent
    R4.7bn to 350 small landlords (40,000 homes); Bitprop has built 506
    backyard flats; Indlu funds backyard rooms (CDE; Bitprop).

  4. The builders
  - Construction employs about 1.2 million people, 38% informally
    ("likely higher"; ILO, Nov 2024). Small and micro contractors are
    now over half of construction employment (Business Day, 16 Jul
    2026). Both are in v1_14_SOURCES.txt, VERIFIED.
  - Government warns homeowners about rogue builders (unfinished homes,
    builders who vanish after payment) and tells them to keep payment
    records (Vuk'uzenzele, Jun 2026).


HOW IT MAPS TO WHAT WE BUILT
  Homes built in stages, paid in cash  -> jobs in stages, each priced
  Nobody can prove what was paid       -> the client signs off each stage
                                          from a link: confirmed by both
  "Did he even build it?"              -> camera-only stage photos, with
                                          our server's time and fingerprint
  No track record beyond word of mouth -> finished jobs become his record
  Cash sales at the rural hardware     -> suppliers who deliver, materials
                                          per job: the store is who pays us
  Patchy signal in villages            -> credit and order books work
                                          offline; JOBS DON'T YET


PITCH LINES
  - "More than 2.3 million families are waiting for a house, and the
    state built 23,000 last year. Everyone else builds for themselves,
    stage by stage, with cash and a local builder."
  - "In Limpopo villages, three in four people who got land in the last
    ten years paid cash. There's no title deed, so there's no mortgage:
    the house goes up in stages, in cash, with no record. Akayza gives
    the family and the builder the record neither has."
  - "The government tells homeowners to keep payment records against
    rogue builders. Akayza is that record, and the honest builder's
    proof of work."


CAREFUL
  - We haven't tested with rural builders; the pilot is the first test.
  - Jobs don't work offline yet (only the credit book and order book).
  - Market sizes are estimates from different sources: say "about" and
    name the source.
  - Never "street vendors". Say owner-builders, small builders, spaza
    shops.


WHAT THIS SUGGESTS WE BUILD NEXT (for Mandla to decide)
  1. Jobs offline, like the credit book (the same pattern: queue, key,
     send once).
  2. The two-phone cash hand-over (QR), so a stage paid in cash is
     confirmed by both without the internet.
  3. A builder's shareable track record: signed-off stages and photos,
     sealed, shown to the next client with the builder's consent.
  4. Hardware stores and brickyards as the first rural suppliers.
