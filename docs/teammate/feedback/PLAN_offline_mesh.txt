PLAN -- recording money offline, phone to phone (the BitChat idea, done
our way)
===========================================================================

BY / DATE:   Risuna, Saturday 26 September 2026 (night)
FOR:         Mandla -- a design for after the hackathon, and an honest
             answer if judges ask "what about places with no signal?"
STATUS:      designed, NOT BUILT. Step 0 below (sealed records) is built.


THE QUESTION
  Much of the informal economy runs on patchy or no signal. Can two
  phones record a sale, a repayment or a cash hand-over with no internet
  at all -- the way BitChat passes messages over Bluetooth -- and still
  end up with a record both sides and our server can trust?


WHAT BITCHAT DOES (checked 26 Sep 2026, github.com/permissionlesstech/bitchat)
  - A Bluetooth Low Energy mesh: phones relay each other's messages, at
    most 7 hops; messages wait in a queue ("store and forward") until a
    route or the internet appears.
  - Encryption with the Noise Protocol Framework, forward secrecy for live
    sessions; a persistent per-device identifier derived from an identity
    key; no accounts or phone numbers.
  - Battery-aware duty cycling; an internet fallback over Nostr relays.
  - Public domain (Unlicense); iOS and Android apps.
  We would borrow the ideas (mesh, store-and-forward, Noise, device keys),
  not the code or the chat product.


WHY IT'S NOT IN THE BUILD TONIGHT (and the rule it would break)
  - Expo can't do Bluetooth in the app we ship today: it needs a native
    BLE module and a development build (EAS or `expo run:android`), and
    CLAUDE.md says we ask before adding native modules.
  - A mesh needs each phone to ADVERTISE and SERVE (BLE peripheral) as
    well as scan; react-native-ble-plx, the usual library, only scans and
    connects (central). Peripheral mode means a small native module of
    our own or another library -- days, not hours.
  - Android 12+ needs BLUETOOTH_SCAN / ADVERTISE / CONNECT permissions and
    limits background scanning; it has to be tested on several real
    phones at once. We have one test phone tonight.
  - A flaky radio demo on stage costs more than it earns. The judges are
    engineers: a design with a working foundation beats a demo that drops.


THE DESIGN, IN STEPS (each step is useful on its own)

  Step 0 -- sealed records                                    BUILT
    POST /me/record/seal: the server fingerprints every record in the
    tools (RFC 6962 Merkle tree) and signs the root with Ed25519 and
    ML-DSA-65 (NIST FIPS 204, post-quantum). The owner keeps the seal;
    "check" shows any sealed record changed or deleted since, even in our
    own database. This is the integrity layer every later step reuses:
    a batch of records carried offline is sealed the same way.

  Step 1 -- a signing key on each phone
    The phone makes an Ed25519 key pair and keeps the private key in its
    secure storage; the server already checks and stores the public key
    (identity/devices, BUILT). Every record made on the phone is signed
    there, so it can't be changed in transit by anyone, including a relay.

  Step 2 -- a handshake by QR code, no radio at all
    The cash hand-over ("I paid the cash") between two phones, offline:
      a) the buyer's phone shows a QR: {order, amount, time, nonce},
         signed with the buyer's device key;
      b) the seller scans it, checks the signature, adds its own
         signature, and shows a QR back;
      c) both phones now hold the same doubly signed record and queue it.
    When either phone gets signal it uploads; the server checks both
    signatures against the registered device keys, the nonce (no replays)
    and the amounts, then records "cash, confirmed by both". Needs only
    the camera (already used for stage photos) and a QR library -- no
    native Bluetooth.

  Step 3 -- the same handshake over Bluetooth, two phones side by side
    A development build with a BLE module: the two phones find each other
    (short range, both apps open), run a Noise XX handshake over their
    device keys, and exchange the same signed record as step 2. Nothing
    else changes: same record, same upload, same server checks.

  Step 4 -- the mesh: carrying records to whoever has signal
    A phone with queued, signed records passes them to nearby phones
    running the app; each relays (at most 7 hops, like BitChat) until one
    has signal and uploads. Relays see only sealed, signed blobs
    (encrypted to our server's key); they can't read, change or forge
    them, and a relayed copy that arrives twice lands once (the record's
    id, as the order book does today).


RULES THAT DON'T CHANGE
  - The server stays the authority: every offline record is re-checked,
    priced and scoped to the signed-in business when it arrives.
  - Offline records are labelled as what they are: "cash, confirmed by
    both" at best -- never proof of payment (CLAUDE.md, honest proof).
  - No personal data travels through other people's phones in the clear.
  - Every step gets the same tests we use now: forged signatures, replays,
    wrong amounts, another business's records (IDOR).


WHAT I NEED FROM MANDLA
  A yes to the direction; then the order of steps 1-2 against "I paid the
  cash" (migration 0024), and a yes before any native module (step 3).
