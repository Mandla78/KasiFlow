Round 2 has one idea behind it:

  THE TOOLS ARE HOW WE LEARN WHAT AN INFORMAL BUSINESS NEEDS.
  A kota shop's order book knows how many quarter loaves it goes through;
  a builder's jobs know what's being built, where, and at which stage.
  That data fuels the SUPPLIER RECOMMENDATION ENGINE (Mandla's side).

  We recommend SUPPLIERS, not products (we are not a marketplace). Inside
  a supplier's catalogue, the products shown first are the ones that
  move most for THIS trader: from their tools and their past orders.

So every tool must be more than CRUD: fast for the trader today, and a
clean, private source of "what this business uses" for the engine.

1. HISTORY + BIN (credit book and jobs)
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

  WHAT THE TRADER SEES
    - HISTORY: a list of what's finished (credit paid back / cancelled;
      jobs done), newest first, searchable by name, with the date. A tab
      or a row "History" on each tool's main screen.
    - DELETE moves an item to the BIN (a row at the bottom of History):
      "Moved to the bin. Undo" (snackbar, 5 s). In the bin: Restore, and
      it says "Kept for 30 days, then removed from your view."
    - A deleted item disappears from lists, Home, Account and totals.

  WHAT REALLY HAPPENS (data is never lost)
    - Delete = hidden from THE USER only. The row stays in the database
      (BaseModel.soft_delete(): is_deleted + deleted_at). After 30 days
      in the bin it is still kept, just no longer shown in the bin
      either (a "binned_at" + "purged_from_view_at", or just compute
      from deleted_at). Never a real DELETE.
    - Why keep it: it's part of the trader's record (what was sold on
      credit, what a client confirmed). A deleted job must not erase a
      stage the client signed off; a deleted credit entry must not erase
      a payment. Audit every delete and restore (no names, no phones).
    - Rules: a stage confirmed by both, or a credit entry with payments,
      can be binned (hidden) but its confirmed parts stay in the record
      and in any shared record link. Say so in the confirm sheet.

  API (propose in CONTRACT_bin.txt): DELETE moves to the bin,
  POST .../restore, GET .../bin, GET .../history?q=. All scoped to
  current_user(), rate-limited, audited.

2. ORDER BOOK (new tool) -- kota shops and food sellers first
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

  WHO: kota shops, food stalls, anyone who takes CUSTOMER orders at a
  counter (not orders to suppliers -- that's "Order stock", Mandla's).
  It must be FAST and CLEAR: a queue of hungry people, one person
  cooking and taking money.

  SCREENS (frontend first, mock API)
    MENU (set up once, edit any time)
      Items with a price: "Kota: chips, polony, cheese R35", "Russian
      kota R45", "Chips small R15", "Cold drink R12"... Starter menus per
      business type so setup is 30 seconds. Optional per item: its main
      ingredients (quarter loaf, chips, polony, russian, cheese, egg...)
      -- this is what feeds the engine (section 4).
    NEW ORDER (the main screen: 2-3 taps)
      Big buttons for the menu items, tap to add (tap again = +1), a
      running total, "Paid cash" / "Paid card or EFT" / "Pay later"
      (pay later -> offers to put it in the credit book). Order gets a
      short number for the day: #1, #2, ...
    QUEUE
      Today's orders: New -> Preparing -> Ready -> Collected, one tap to
      move each. Big number, what's in it, how long it's been waiting.
    TODAY
      Orders, money in (cash vs digital), best sellers, busiest hour.
      Week view: the same per day. This is the trader's own value.

  RULES
    - Works when the signal drops: orders saved on the phone first, sent
      when back online with an Idempotency-Key each (the app header is
      coming from Mandla: feedback/NEEDS_api_client_headers.txt). Two
      phones in one shop must not create duplicate order numbers
      (the server assigns the final number; the phone shows a temporary
      one until then) -- propose how in the contract.
    - No customer details needed. An optional first name for the queue
      only ("Thabo's order"), never a phone number unless the owner
      turns on "tell customers when it's ready" (later).
    - Money in cents; prices from the menu, totals computed on the
      server; a price change never changes past orders (snapshot, like
      Mandla's order lines).
    - Tool key: "orderBook". Mandla adds it to constants/businessTypes.ts
      and the Add-a-tool sheet (write a NEEDS_ file with the label and
      icon you want; default ON for a "food seller" type).

  BACKEND (after the contract is approved)
    trader.order_book_items (menu), trader.order_book_orders,
    trader.order_book_lines (snapshot name/price), per-trader rate
    limits, idempotent writes, audit (no names). Tests: another trader
    can't see or change your orders; retries land once; hostile input.

4. SIGNALS FOR THE ENGINE (small; Mandla wires it)
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Each tool exposes ONE read-only service function in its own domain
  (never a repository; the domain-boundary tests enforce it):

    order_book_service.demand_signals(user) ->
      {"categories": {"bakery": 0.4, "meat_frozen": 0.3, ...},   # share
       "weekly_units": {"quarter_loaf": 140, "chips_kg": 12, ...},
       "since": "2026-09-01"}
    jobs_service.demand_signals(user) ->
      {"categories": {"building_materials": 0.6, "plumbing": 0.2},
       "active_sites": [{"lat": -26.0, "lng": 28.2}],   # where to deliver
       "next_stages": ["walls", "roof"]}

  PRIVACY (non-negotiable): signals are the trader's own data used for
  the trader's own suggestions. Aggregated, no customer or client names,
  no phone numbers, never sent to a supplier. The app says it in plain
  words: "Your tools help us suggest suppliers for what you use."

  HOW MANDLA USES THEM (so you know why): suppliers who sell those
  categories and deliver to those sites rank higher, with a reason line
  ("You sold 140 kotas last week; Kasi Bakers delivers quarter loaves");
  inside the supplier's catalogue, products matching those categories
  and the trader's past orders come first.
