A. GROUND RULES FOR ANSWERING
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

  - One or two sentences first, then stop. Offer more: "Want the detail?"
  - Never claim what isn't built. "Designed, and it's next" is a strong
    answer; a claim a judge can check and break is not.
  - Always say "spaza shops and builders" (and other informal
    businesses). Never "street vendors".
  - Not a marketplace: we recommend SUPPLIERS, never products.
  - Only a digital payment verified by the payment provider is proof.
    Cash is at most "confirmed by both". The tools are the business's own
    record. Never add them together; never say "proof" about tool data.
  - "Where": nationwide, all nine provinces. The demo places (Tembisa,
    Isekelo, Polokwane) are test data.
  - Don't know? "I don't have that number with me; what I can tell you
    is ..." -- then something true.
  - Split the room: Mandla takes the business, the supplier side and
    payments; Risuna takes the tools, the engine signals and security
    details. Hand over by name ("Risuna built that part").

    Not how it works: every NEW phone needs a code by email; trusted

  "Both sides confirm the cash" on ORDERS
    Not yet: that's "I paid the cash" (Mandla, in progress). Today an
    order's cash shows "not confirmed". It IS built for jobs (the client

  "Download my data / delete my account" in the app
    Built and tested on the server; the app shows "coming soon". [8]
  "Records can't be changed" (in general)
    Say exactly what: after the owner SEALS their record, any change or

  languages, Google sign-in, malware scanning -- all "next", not live.

D. PROBLEM AND USERS
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Q: Who exactly is this for?
  A: Spaza shops and builders first -- and other informal businesses --
     on Android, mostly cash, buying from 2-5 regular suppliers, in all
     nine provinces. The businesses use it free; the suppliers who serve
     them pay.

  Q: Why would a spaza owner use this instead of a notebook and WhatsApp?
  A: It does the notebook's job faster -- who owes me, with a reminder
     that opens their own WhatsApp -- and they can order from the
     suppliers they already use, with an invoice and a receipt. It's
     free, and the history builds itself as they work.
     If they push: the order book works with no signal; balances are
     hidden when the app opens, so it's safe at the counter.

  Q: And a builder?
  A: Jobs in stages the client signs off from a link -- no app, no
     account -- with camera-only stage photos. It ends "you said you'd
     pay, I say you didn't" arguments, and they can bring in a partner
     with the pay agreed up front.

  Q: Have you spoken to real users?
  A: Say only what really happened: who, how many, what they said. If
     not yet at scale: "Not at scale. The pilot starts with two suppliers
     and the 50-100 businesses they already serve -- that's our first
     real test."

  Q: Data costs? Bad signal?
  A: Light by design: small requests, images resized before they reach
     the phone, alerts checked only while the app is open. The order
     book works offline and sends once the signal is back, without
     double orders.
     If they push: full offline for every tool (an encrypted store on
     the phone) is next.

  Q: What about places with no signal at all? Something like BitChat's
     Bluetooth mesh?
  A: The order book already works with no signal and sends later without
     double orders. Phone to phone, we've designed it in steps: records
     signed on the phone, a cash hand-over done by scanning QR codes with
     no network, then Bluetooth between two phones, then a BitChat-style
     mesh that carries sealed, signed records to whoever has signal. The
     server re-checks everything when it arrives.
     If they push "why not tonight?": Bluetooth needs a native module and
     a custom build, and a mesh has to be tested on several phones -- a
     radio demo that drops on stage helps nobody. The integrity layer it
     needs, sealed records, is built.

  Q: Languages? People who don't read English well?
  A: English only today, written the way traders speak, with icons and
     big buttons. Other South African languages are on the roadmap.


---------------------------------------------------------------------------
E. INNOVATION
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Q: Isn't this just another marketplace or point-of-sale app?
  A: No. We never sell or push products; we help a business work with
     the suppliers it already trusts. What's new: the everyday tools
     privately tell the supplier engine what the business needs NOW.
     A builder with plumbing stages open sees plumbing suppliers rise,
     with "Your open jobs need plumbing" on screen.

  Q: How does the recommendation work? Is it AI?
  A: It's a clear formula, not a black box: what you buy, whether they
     reach you, how close, how you pay, whether their minimum fits your
     spend -- plus what your tools say you need now. Every supplier is
     ranked with its reasons; nobody is hidden and nobody pays to rank
     higher. [5.3]
     If they push "why not machine learning?": we have no data yet, and
     traders trust what they can read. When there's data, the model can
     learn the weights -- and still show its reasons.

  Q: Competitors?
  A: Each does one slice: Spazapp (bulk buying), Kasi App (point of
     sale), iKhokha (card payments), Kazang and Flash (airtime and
     electricity float), Kasi Money (banking), HustleCheck (reviews). We
     join the everyday tools to the business's own suppliers, and we're
     honest about what each rand is backed by.

  Q: What stops a big player copying you?
  A: The data only exists because the tools are used every day, and it
     stays private to the business; the trust that comes from honest
     labels; the suppliers we onboard; and building for cash-first,
     low-signal businesses from day one.


---------------------------------------------------------------------------
F. HOW IT'S BUILT
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Q: Walk us through the architecture.
  A: A React Native app (Expo, TypeScript) talks over HTTPS to our own
     API (Python, Flask) and a PostgreSQL database. The backend is split
     into areas -- identity, the business tools, suppliers, commerce,
     notifications -- and a test fails the build if one area reaches into
     another's data. PayFast, Cloudinary, Mapbox and email sit behind
     one module each. [2, 3]

  Q: How do you know it works?
  A: About 1,240 backend and 196 app tests, run before every merge --
     including attacks: injection, broken input, one business trying to
     read another's data, forged payment notices. They found real bugs,
     which we fixed. [9]

  Q: How does it scale?
  A: The API is stateless, so it runs behind a load balancer on more
     servers; the database is a managed PostgreSQL. What changes at
     scale: rate limits move to a shared store (Redis), background work
     to a proper worker queue, and the engine caches its results. The
     pilot costs about R460 a month to run.

  Q: Why your own backend and not Firebase or a no-code tool?
  A: Money, stock and proof rules have to live on a server we control:
     the phone never sets a price or a "paid", and the audit trail can't
     be edited. A relational database gives us constraints, atomic stock
     and triggers, and we can host in South Africa for POPIA.

  Q: How do suppliers get on?
  A: Their catalogue comes in as a file (a profile plus a product
     spreadsheet), checked row by row: prices, barcodes, no spreadsheet
     formulas, images only from our own image service. 11 suppliers and
     about 1,670 products in the demo. [5.4, 7]

  Q: How does a supplier receive and accept an order?
  A: Honest answer: in production, from their own system through our
     supplier API -- which is designed, not built. In the demo we move
     the order as the supplier would. The buyer's side, payment and
     documents are real. [5.4]

  Q: What if two phones use one account? What if the signal drops mid-order?
  A: Each phone has its own session. "Place order" carries a key, so a
     retry returns the first order instead of making a second one. Order
     book orders made offline carry the phone's own id, kept unique per
     business, so a resend lands once.


---------------------------------------------------------------------------
G. EASE OF USE AND DESIGN
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Q: Won't all this security confuse non-technical users?
  A: Most of it is invisible. On a phone they've used before, the
     password is enough -- the email code only comes on a new phone. A
     client confirms a stage from a WhatsApp link with no account and no
     app. Plain words, no jargon.

  Q: Why are the balances hidden?
  A: A shop owner opens the app at the counter, in front of customers.
     Amounts stay hidden until they tap the eye, and the choice is
     remembered on that phone only.

  Q: Why tools you switch on?
  A: A spaza doesn't need jobs; a builder doesn't need an order book.
     They switch on what their business uses, and can change it any time.

  Q: Accessibility?
  A: Buttons carry screen-reader labels and the tap targets are big
     (44 points). A full accessibility review is on the list.


---------------------------------------------------------------------------
H. SECURITY, PRIVACY AND ETHICS
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Q: How do you protect accounts?
  A: Passwords hashed with bcrypt, lockout after 5 tries, a code by email
     on any new phone, 15-minute sessions that renew with one-time
     tokens -- reuse one and the session ends. Nobody can find out which
     emails have accounts. [5.1]

  Q: What if a phone is stolen?
  A: The thief still needs the password; amounts are hidden by default.
     From another phone the owner removes the trusted phone or changes
     the password, which ends every session at once.

  Q: Can one business see another's data?
  A: No. Every query is limited to the signed-in business, ids are
     random, and someone else's record answers "not found", exactly like
     one that doesn't exist. Tested for every feature. [7]

  Q: Can someone fake a payment?
  A: An order is marked paid only when PayFast's own server tells us --
     and that message must pass four checks: our signature, PayFast's
     servers, the exact amount, and PayFast confirming it when we ask
     back. The phone can't send a price or a "paid". [5.5]

  Q: Do you store card details?
  A: Never. The buyer pays on PayFast's own page; we keep the amount,
     the status and PayFast's reference -- the smallest card-security
     scope a merchant can have.

  Q: The business can type anything into its books. Isn't the record
     worthless?
  A: That's exactly why we never call it proof. Only a digital payment
     PayFast verified is proof; cash is "confirmed by both" at best; the
     credit book and jobs say "your own record". They're shown apart and
     never added together. [6]

  Q: POPIA?
  A: We collect only what a feature needs or what can be checked;
     consent is recorded at sign-up; customers' names never leave the
     business's own books; the engine uses totals, not people; no names
     or numbers in alerts or logs; export and account closure are built
     on the server; South African hosting at launch; a POPIA legal
     review is in our ask. [8]

  Q: What if your database leaks?
  A: No card data at all; passwords only as bcrypt hashes; codes and
     link tickets only as hashes. What would leak is business details
     and names in the books -- so access is tight, the audit trail can't
     be edited, and the incident plan is: revoke sessions, rotate keys,
     tell the people affected and the Information Regulator (POPIA
     section 22).

  Q: Couldn't this be used against traders -- lenders, tax, suppliers
     squeezing them?
  A: The owner owns the record. Nothing is shared without them -- when
     sharing comes, it's their choice and they can take it back.
     Suppliers never see customers' names or the business's books, and
     can't pay to rank higher. The core tools stay free.

  Q: How much of this did AI write? Can you trust it?
  A: We used AI assistance and say so. Every change goes through a
     branch, a review by a team member and the full test suite before
     merge, under written security rules. We can explain any part of it.

  Q: Can you show that a business's records weren't changed later --
     even by you?
  A: Yes. The owner taps "Seal my record": we fingerprint every record
     in their tools, build a Merkle tree and sign the root twice -- Ed25519
     and ML-DSA-65, the NIST post-quantum standard. The owner keeps the
     seal; we keep no copy. "Check my record" names any record changed or
     deleted since -- even if the change was made straight in our
     database -- and our public keys are published so anyone can check a
     seal without us. [5.10]
     If they push: a seal proves nothing changed since sealing, not that
     the records are true. Proof of payment is still only PayFast's.

  Q: What was your best security catch?
  A: Behind a proxy, every caller looks like one address -- one attacker
     could use up everyone's sign-in attempts, and the audit trail
     trusted a header anyone can fake. We fixed it with one trusted
     client-address helper and 19 tests. Also: deeply nested JSON
     crashed every route, found by our own hostile tests, fixed.


---------------------------------------------------------------------------
I. MONEY: WHO PAYS, COSTS, THE ASK
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Q: Who pays, and how much?
  A: Businesses never pay for the core tools. Suppliers pay R299 or
     R1,499 a month, plus about 1% on digital orders through the app --
     nothing on cash. Integration with a supplier's own system comes
     later at R4,999 a month plus setup.

  Q: Why would a supplier pay?
  A: More orders from the shops and builders they already serve, placed
     properly: priced from their catalogue, with invoices and receipts
     done for them, cash orders capped (a fake order can't cost them
     much), and digital payments confirmed by PayFast. Nearby businesses that buy
     what they sell see them ranked honestly -- they can't buy a better
     spot.

  Q: How will you get users?
  A: Through the suppliers: each one brings the businesses it already
     serves -- at the counter, on the driver's route, on its invoices. A
     business can also invite the suppliers it buys from, and builders
     bring partners onto jobs.

  Q: Costs and break-even?
  A: About R460 a month to run the pilot; two suppliers on Starter
     cover it. At growth about R4,000-R4,700 a month, about four on Pro.

  Q: What's the R300,000 for?
  A: A 6-month pilot: a field agent, small stipends and data bundles for
     the pilot businesses, running costs, a POPIA legal review, an
     external security test, test phones, and a contingency.

  Q: How will you know the pilot worked?
  A: 60% of the pilot businesses active every week, 30% of their orders
     to the pilot suppliers placed in the app, half of those paid
     digitally, and under 1% disputed.

  Q: Who pays PayFast's fees?
  A: PayFast charges its own fee on digital payments; our 1% is
     separate, and cash carries no fee from us. (If pressed on the exact
     split, say it's being set with the pilot suppliers.)


---------------------------------------------------------------------------
J. QUANTUM
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Q: Where's the quantum in this?
  A: Record seals are signed with ML-DSA-65, the NIST post-quantum
     signature standard (FIPS 204), alongside Ed25519. A business's
     records must stay checkable for years -- tax law keeps them five --
     and a quantum computer that could forge Ed25519 couldn't forge
     ML-DSA. Signing with both means a seal stays checkable if either
     scheme ever falls. Show it: seal, change one number in the database,
     check -- it names the record. [5.10]
     (Only if the sealed-records pull request is merged. If it isn't,
     say: "outlined, and built on a branch".)
     If they push on speed: pure Python, about 85 ms to sign and 13 ms to
     check; keys are derived from one server secret with HKDF.
     If they push on transport: Cloudflare's tunnel already prefers the
     hybrid post-quantum key exchange X25519MLKEM768 -- we saw it in our
     tunnel logs -- and our launch plan puts the API behind Cloudflare.
     Don't say: "post-quantum secure", "quantum-proof", or that we use
     quantum computing.

L. HARD QUESTIONS
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Q: Your demo shows a sandbox. What's actually live?
  A: The whole buyer side runs on our real server: sign-up, suppliers,
     ordering, PayFast's sandbox payment with the real checks, invoices,
     receipts, and every business tool. Simulated: the supplier moving
     the order, and the company check. Not built: the supplier API and
     record sharing.

  Q: Why should a funder trust a record from your app?
  A: They should trust exactly what we label: PayFast-verified payments
     are independent proof; cash confirmed by both shows both sides
     agreed; the rest is the owner's own record. We never mix them --
     that honesty is the product.

  Q: What happens when a supplier and a business disagree about cash?
  A: Until both sides confirm, cash stays "not confirmed" and never
     counts as proof. For orders, the two-sided confirmation ("I paid the
     cash") is being finished now. For jobs it's built: the client types
     what they paid, and if it differs the stage says "Amounts don't
     match".

  Q: What's your biggest risk?
  A: Getting suppliers to pay. That's why onboarding runs through them,
     the pilot starts with two, and they break even for us early.
     Second: trust in cash records -- which is why we label them
     honestly.

  Q: What would you build next with more time?
  A: The supplier API, split payouts to suppliers, sharing a sealed
     record with a funder (owner-approved, revocable), the offline
     hand-over (QR, then Bluetooth), and South African languages.

  Q: Who are you, and why you?
  A: Two developers who built and tested all of this in the hackathon,
     with a security-first process; the team won LP Dev DataQuest 2026.


---------------------------------------------------------------------------
M. IF THE DEMO BREAKS
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Before going on stage
    - Backend running; the tunnel up; the backend's public address
      matches the tunnel (a quick tunnel changes its address when it
      restarts -- ours expired on Saturday night).
    - Sign in on the demo phone beforehand, so no email code is needed
      on stage (a trusted phone skips it).
    - Seed data loaded (suppliers, builders, the tools demo).
    - A terminal ready with `flask orders move AKZ-2026-... accepted`.
    - For the tamper moment: seal on the phone first; then
      `flask tools tamper --me EMAIL`, then "Check my record".
    - The recorded demo clips on the laptop as a backup.

  During
    - Payment page won't open: "The payment runs on PayFast's page; here
      is the confirmed result" -- switch to the recorded clip.
    - No signal: the order book works offline -- show it; or run the app
      in mock mode (EXPO_PUBLIC_USE_MOCK_API=true), which shows every
      screen on sample data (no real payment).
    - Something odd: say what it should do and move on. Never pretend
      it worked.
