1. THE NAME
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

AKAYZA. "Kasi" places it in the track in one word. "Flow" is what the
product does: stock flowing in from suppliers, money flowing in from
customers, and a record flowing out of both without anyone typing a
report.

Tagline options (pick one for slide 1):
    "Every kasi hustle, in flow."
    "Run your hustle. Keep the proof."
    "From your supplier to your customer, and proof of both."

Before it goes on a slide: CIPC name search, a trademark search, and

but nearby names exist ("Kasi App", "Kasi Money", "Kas Flow").


2. THE PITCH, IN FOUR SENTENCES
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    The kasi economy isn't one business. It's the spaza, the builder, the
    fruit seller at the rank and the student selling fragrances from a
    backpack, and every one of them runs on cash, WhatsApp and a
    notebook.

    Akayza gives each of them the tools for how their kind of business
    actually works: an Order Book, a Loan Book, ordering from the
    suppliers they already use, and a Market that shows what's moving
    around them.

    When cash changes hands between two Akayza users, both phones
    confirm the amount over Bluetooth, on the spot, with no signal. We
    call it the Cash Handshake.

    Everything they do builds a record that they own, which proves
    their business is real to the next supplier, client or fund that
    asks.


3. WHY THIS WINS
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

The judging frame is "Build for Use": identified users, a validated
problem, security-by-design, a sustainability plan, measurable impact.

  A. IT'S BUILT FOR THE TYPES, NOT FOR "THE INFORMAL SECTOR"
     Most apps for this market pick one type (usually the spaza) and
     treat everyone else as an edge case. Akayza starts with "what kind
     of business are you?" and arranges the same small set of tools
     around the answer. A builder sees jobs and stage payments. A spaza
     sees stock and credit. A reseller sees orders and who still owes.
     One app, one data model, many ways of working. See V1_03, section 2.

  B. USEFUL WITH ZERO SUPPLIERS, BETTER WITH THEM
     The Order Book and Loan Book work on day one, alone. Suppliers make
     it better: ordering, deliveries and the Cash Handshake. Nobody is
     told "connect a supplier to continue".

  C. SUPPLIERS PAY, BUSINESSES DON'T
     A clear sustainability answer, and a channel at the same time.
     A wholesaler that onboards its own spaza customers gets their
     orders in one place and their payments confirmed. Every supplier
     brings dozens of businesses with it.

  D. CASH HANDSHAKE: THE LIVE MOMENT
     Two phones on camera, no signal, both confirming R2,340 on
     delivery. Then a deliberately wrong amount becomes a dispute live.
     Then a tampered record breaks its hash chain. A cybersecurity panel
     remembers that sequence.

  E. THE GOVERNMENT IS ASKING FOR THE RECORD
     The Spaza Shop Support Fund lost 354 applications to verification
     failures. Government advice to homeowners hiring builders is to
     "keep payment records". Akayza is how a cash business produces
     one. The co-host, the Centre for Public Service Innovation, will
     recognise both.


4. WHAT IT IS NOT
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    - Not a lender and not a wallet. We hold no money and grant no
      credit. A supplier may offer its own customers terms. That's the
      supplier's decision.
    - Not a marketplace that invents relationships. It digitises the
      supplier relationships businesses already have, and helps them
      find more.
    - Not bookkeeping for somebody else's benefit. Every tool answers a
      question the owner already asks: who owes me, what do I owe, what
      should I reorder.


5. HOW IT MAPS TO THE THEMES
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    MAIN THEME -- "Build for Use: Igniting Capabilities for Markets"
        Both sides of a real market, businesses and their suppliers, on
        phones they already own, working offline where trade happens.

    TRACK -- Kasi and Street Economy
        Every type in the track, not just one of them.

    SECURITY-BY-DESIGN
        Role-pinned API, the server trusts no client claim, Ed25519 hash
        chains, salted Bluetooth commitments, independent amount entry,
        owner-held data, expiring share links. See V1_05.

    SUSTAINABILITY
        Free for informal businesses, always. Suppliers pay: a
        subscription for the supplier tools, clearly labelled sponsored
        placement in the Market, and (with the business's consent)
        verified reports when offering terms.

    MEASURABLE IMPACT (pilot metrics)
        - businesses active weekly, by type
        - share of cash handovers confirmed by Cash Handshake
        - disputes raised on the spot vs. discovered later
        - time from order to delivery; share of orders completed
        - suppliers offering terms on the strength of a record

7. WHAT WE DO NOT CLAIM
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    - A Cash Handshake proves the two registered counterparties both
      signed the same amount within 60 seconds of each other. It
      doesn't prove the notes were real, that nobody was pressured, or
      that the phones were in the same place: radio packets can be
      relayed, and a QR code can travel as a screenshot. (rev 4)
    - A hash chain proves nothing changed after it was sealed, not that
      it was true when entered.
    - The reputation describes a track record. It doesn't predict
      repayment, and there is no outcome data yet.
