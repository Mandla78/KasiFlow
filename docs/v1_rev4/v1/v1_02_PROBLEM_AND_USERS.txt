1. THE PROBLEM
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

The kasi economy runs on three things: CASH, WHATSAPP AND A NOTEBOOK.

    - Orders to suppliers go by phone call or WhatsApp. Prices get
      re-agreed every time, and the argument at delivery is one memory
      against another.
    - Credit to customers ("I'll pay you Friday") lives in a notebook
      that gets lost, wet or disputed.
    - Cash changes hands with no receipt: at the wholesaler's counter,
      on delivery, in a yard where a homeowner pays a builder's deposit.
    - At the end of the month the owner doesn't know their own numbers.
    - And when it matters (a fund, a supplier deciding on terms, a new
      client choosing a builder) there is nothing to show.

Read the chain backwards and the fix is obvious:

    no access    <- no evidence   <- no record   <- no tool worth using
                                                    for MY kind of business

Almost everything built for this market attacks the first link
(scoring, lending, verification visits) or builds a tool for one type
of business. Akayza attacks the last link, for every type.


2. THE EVIDENCE (sources in V1_14; VERIFIED items only on slides)
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    - ~R900 billion township economy; nearly 80% of township businesses
      surveyed unregistered; fewer than 9% with access to bank loans
      (Standard Bank Township Informal Economy Report, 2025).
    - Informal employment is 19.5% of total employment (Stats SA, QLFS
      Q4 2024).
    - Spaza Shop Support Fund: 354 applications could not proceed after
      site visits (non-existent businesses, ownership discrepancies,
      applicant-operator mismatches). Only R179.6m of R500m had been
      allocated by May 2026.
    - Builders: government guidance (Vuk'uzenzele, June 2026) warns of
      rogue builders who "disappear after receiving payment" and tells
      homeowners to keep payment records.
    - Small and micro enterprises now employ over half the construction
      sector, and finance is one of their biggest obstacles (Business
      Day, July 2026).
    - Cash still dominates township trade (several 2025 reports), but
      with no reliable percentage. Say "dominates", not a number.


3. THE USERS -- INFORMAL BUSINESSES, BY TYPE
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

The types we design for this weekend. Each one is a real way of
working, not a persona:

  SPAZA / TUCK SHOP / GENERAL DEALER -- "Nomsa, Tembisa"
      Buys stock weekly from a wholesaler. Sells some of it on credit to
      neighbours. Needs: reorder fast, know who owes what, and confirm
      cash paid to the wholesaler on delivery.

  BUILDER / TRADESPERSON -- "Sipho, Seshego"
      Builder, plumber, electrician, tiler, roofer, painter, welder.
      Takes jobs, collects a deposit and stage payments in cash, and buys
      materials from a hardware store. Needs: jobs and stages, receipts
      the client can't argue with, and a record of finished work to win
      the next client.

  RESELLER / SIDE HUSTLE -- "Lerato, a student in Polokwane"
      Sells fragrances (or clothes, or airtime) to other students. Buys
      from a distributor, takes orders on WhatsApp, and half the
      customers pay later. Needs: who ordered what, who's paid, and
      what to reorder.

  FOOD VENDOR / STREET TRADER (lighter support this weekend)
      Daily cash takings, daily stock. Needs: a daily number, and
      reorders.

What they share is exactly what Akayza's tools are: orders in and
out, credit given, cash received, stock bought. Only the words and the
order of the screens differ by type.


4. THE CUSTOMERS -- SUPPLIERS (they pay)
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

  SPAZA WHOLESALER / CASH-AND-CARRY -- "Mahlangu Wholesale"
      Serves dozens of spazas. Takes orders by phone. Delivers, or the
      shop collects. Often paid cash on delivery.

  HARDWARE / BUILDING SUPPLY STORE -- "Ndlovu Hardware & Building"
      Sells to builders daily. Some builders ask for materials on
      account.

  DISTRIBUTOR -- fragrances, cosmetics, airtime, clothing
      Serves resellers, often through WhatsApp groups.

What a supplier gets from Akayza:
    - orders from their existing customers arrive in one list, not
      forty WhatsApp chats
    - cash on delivery confirmed by both phones (Cash Handshake), so
      the driver's cash reconciles
    - a customer list: who orders, how often, who's gone quiet
    - (with the business's consent) a customer's record, when deciding
      whether to offer terms
    - reach: new businesses of the right type near them, through the
      Market

Why suppliers pay: those five things replace a phone, a notebook, a
WhatsApp group and a gut feeling, for their whole customer base.


5. HOW IT SPREADS
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    SUPPLIER-LED. A wholesaler or hardware store invites its own
    customers: "order from us on Akayza". One supplier brings dozens
    of businesses, and each business gets the full toolset free.

    BUSINESS-LED. A builder asks each client to confirm payments. A
    spaza asks its wholesaler to take orders on Akayza. Each
    business pulls its counterparties in.

    TYPE-LED. Students sell to students, and builders know builders.
    The types have their own word of mouth.


6. VALIDATION -- BEFORE FRIDAY (V1_12)
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

Target: 2 spaza owners, 1 builder, 1 student reseller, 1 wholesaler,
1 hardware store. Voice notes are fine. Ask permission to quote them by
first name.

ANY BUSINESS
    1. How do you order stock? How do you pay for it?
    2. Who owes you money right now? How do you keep track?
    3. Has a supplier or customer ever disputed what was paid? What
       happened?
    4. Have you ever needed to prove your business is real? What did you
       show?
    5. If both phones confirmed a cash payment on the spot, would you
       use it?

SUPPLIERS
    1. How do your customers order? How many messages a day is that?
    2. How do you reconcile cash your driver collects on delivery?
    3. Do you give any customer terms? How do you decide?
    4. What would you pay for a tool that fixed one of the three above?
       (Ask it plainly. The answer is the sustainability slide.)

Anything that contradicts the plan goes into V1_13 and changes what we
say.
