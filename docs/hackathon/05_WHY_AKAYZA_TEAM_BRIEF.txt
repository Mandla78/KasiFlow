1. THE IDEA IN ONE BREATH
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

Akayza is a business app for spaza shops (builders second). The
trader runs the business on it: a credit book, stock orders from the
suppliers they ALREADY buy from (paid by PayFast, or cash both sides
confirm), and a tamper-evident record of their trading that they own
and can share to prove the business is real.

THE INSIGHT: informal businesses already know who they buy from. So
Akayza is NOT a marketplace and doesn't recommend products. It makes
the existing supplier relationship digital, paid and provable.

WHAT KEEPS TRADERS COMING BACK: the BUSINESS TOOLS. The credit book,
reordering and the record are useful every day, even before a single
supplier joins. That daily use is the engine; everything else grows
from it.

4. HOW SUPPLIERS JOIN -- THREE WAYS
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

  1. INVITES FROM TRADERS (the easiest): a spaza already uses Akayza
    for its own tools. It taps "Invite my supplier", and the supplier
    gets an EMAIL (and/or WhatsApp) from Akayza: "Nomsa's Spaza
    wants to order from you on Akayza", with two buttons, [Join
    Akayza] and [Contact us]. The supplier comes in warm, because its
    own customer asked.
  2. SALES: we also sell directly to suppliers (wholesalers,
    distributors, brands), especially the big ones with ERPs, where a
    sales conversation and an integration are needed anyway.
  3. SUPPLIER-LED (it compounds): once a supplier is on, it invites
    the rest of the shops it serves. One wholesaler can bring ~50 shops
    (est.).
  PILOT (to start the loop): one wholesaler + its shops, run by us.

  HOW A SUPPLIER RUNS ITS SIDE
    - Has an ERP / system    -> the integration API. Nothing new to
                                learn.
    - Has no system          -> a Akayza SUPPLIER TABLET: an Android
                                tablet with the supplier app (orders,
                                products, stock, payments, drivers).
                                Included on the Pro plan if they want
                                one (est. tablet cost ~R2,500, recovered
                                over a 12-month plan).
    (The tablet app is roadmap; this weekend suppliers are connected
    through the backend and the integration API.)

6. VALUE-ADDED SERVICES (VAS) -- RESEARCHED, FOR THE ROADMAP
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

  WHAT SPAZAS DO TODAY: many already sell airtime, data and
  electricity through Kazang, Flash or Blu Approved terminals and apps.
  They work on a prepaid FLOAT: when it runs out, the owner reloads it
  by bank deposit, EFT, instant EFT (Ozow, on Kazang), a cash deposit
  at a vendor, or a top-up voucher, and some wholesalers act as Kazang
  top-up points. So topping up is a trip or a separate app, and none of
  it shows up in their books.

  THE AKAYZA ANGLE (complement Kazang/Flash, don't fight them)
    1. TOP UP THE FLOAT INSIDE AKAYZA: when the airtime/electricity
       float runs low, the trader reloads it from Akayza (like
       ordering stock from a supplier), through a partnership with the
       float provider. No trip to the bank, no separate app.
    2. SELL VAS FROM AKAYZA: airtime, data and electricity sold to a
       customer straight from the app, through an aggregator API.
       Aggregators with developer APIs exist: Prepaid24 Corporate API,
       Powertime VAS, Blu Label Distribution, and Nedbank's VAS API.
       Akayza earns a share of the commission.
    3. It counts in the business tools automatically: those sales add
       to the day's takings on Home, the same way a credit sale lands
       in the credit book, so the owner sees their real daily numbers
       without writing anything down.
  NEXT STEP: ask 3 spaza owners how they reload their float, and how
  often it runs out. Talk to one aggregator about partner terms.
  NOT built this weekend: a roadmap slide.

  WHAT THEY ASKED FOR, AND US
    "Would a real user trust and use this?" -> it digitises what
      traders already do, and the tools are useful from day one.
    "Less AI reliance" -> no AI at all.
    "Consumer-facing" -> the trader is the user, on their own phone.
    "Business sense with figures" -> sections 5 and 7.

  "BUT 96 TEAMS PICKED STREET ECONOMY" -- the worry, and the answer:
    - Competition exists in every market. The best product wins, and
      the best SALES wins. That's true on Sunday and after.
    - Most of them will build something a trader tries once: a
      directory, a marketplace, a rating page. What makes people KEEP
      using an app is using it every day for their own business. Our
      business tools (credit book, reorders, cash confirmed, the
      record) are that daily habit. The supplier side and the money
      grow on top of it. That's what they won't have.
    - We're not even in their challenge: we're on Brand New, judged
      on the criteria, not against 96 look-alikes.
  KEEP IT PRIVATE UNTIL WE PRESENT: on Sonke and anywhere public, we
    use a CODENAME, not "Akayza", and no description of the idea
    until we're ready. The real name appears on Sunday.

  THE OTHER TEAMS: 96 on Street Economy, 98 on Brand New; many will
  pitch "an app for informal traders". We lead with what only we do:
  your existing suppliers (not a marketplace), cash confirmed by both
  sides, and a record the trader owns.
  Existing products each do one slice: Spazapp (bulk buying), Kasi
  App (POS), iKhokha (card payments / POS), Kazang and Flash (VAS
