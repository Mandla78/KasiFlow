--- BACKGROUND: What inspired / motivated this project? -------------------

South Africa's township economy is worth about R900 billion a year, and
spaza shops alone about R180 billion. Yet nearly four in five township
businesses are unregistered and fewer than 9% can get a bank loan
(Standard Bank Township Informal Economy Report, 2025). We grew up
around these shops. They run on cash, WhatsApp and a notebook: the
owner phones the same wholesaler every week, pays the driver in cash,
and neither side keeps a record they both agree on.

What pushed us to build: when government opened the R500 million Spaza
Shop Support Fund, 354 applications failed verification, and only
R179.6 million had been allocated by May 2026 (DSBD, 29 May 2026). Real
businesses couldn't prove they were real. We asked what would make
that proof a side effect of running the shop, instead of paperwork.

--- OVERVIEW: What is the project about? ----------------------------------

Akayza is a free business app for spaza shops (and builders) that
turns the supplier relationships they already have into digital,
paid and provable trade.

- Credit book: who owes you, how much, and when they pay.
- Suppliers: the trader's own suppliers, listed; tap one, see its
  products, reorder in a few taps. Not a marketplace: traders already
  know who they buy from.
- Pay by PayFast (straight to the supplier; Akayza never holds money)
  or cash on delivery, where BOTH sides type the amount and the match
  is sealed, even with no signal.
- My record: a tamper-evident trading history the owner controls and
  can share by link (and revoke) with a supplier or a funder.
- Builders: jobs with stages, camera-only stage photos, and a client
  sign-off link that needs no app.

Suppliers pay a monthly subscription; traders use it free.

--- TECHNOLOGIES ------------------------------------------------------------

- Mobile: React Native + Expo (development build), TypeScript, Expo
  Router, SQLite (offline store), expo-secure-store (Android Keystore)
- Backend: Python, Flask, PostgreSQL, SQLAlchemy, JWT auth
- Payments: PayFast (sandbox), with server-verified payment
  notifications
- Media: Cloudinary · Maps: Mapbox · Sign-in: email + Google Sign-In
- Security: Ed25519 signatures, SHA-256 hash chains, ML-DSA
  post-quantum signatures (NIST FIPS 204) on sealed records
- Offline cash confirmation: signed QR codes (Bluetooth as a fallback)

--- THE PROBLEM STATEMENT (max 2 paragraphs) -----------------------------

Spaza shops carry a R180 billion-a-year trade on cash, WhatsApp and a
notebook. Orders to the same wholesaler are re-agreed by phone every
week, cash is paid to drivers with no receipt both sides accept, and
credit to customers lives in a book that gets lost or disputed. Nearly
four in five township businesses are unregistered and fewer than 9%
can get a bank loan (Standard Bank, 2025).

Because nothing is recorded, a real business can't prove it's real:
354 Spaza Shop Support Fund applications failed verification and only
R179.6m of R500m had been allocated by May 2026 (DSBD). Suppliers
suffer too: forty WhatsApp chats of orders, and a driver's cash that
doesn't reconcile.

--- SOLUTION & FEATURES ------------------------------------------------------

Free Android app for spaza owners (and builders):
- Credit book with WhatsApp receipts
- Your suppliers, their products, reorder in a few taps
- Pay by PayFast, or cash confirmed by both sides (works offline)
- Delivery or collection, with the supplier's address and hours
- My record: tamper-evident history, shared by link, revocable
- Builder jobs: stages, photos, client sign-off link
Suppliers are connected through our backend/API; no new system to
learn.

--- KEY METRICS -------------------------------------------------------------

Pilot targets, first 6 months (est.):
- 5 suppliers live, 250 spaza shops active weekly
- 60% of orders paid by PayFast
- 80% of cash handovers confirmed by both sides
- Disputes caught at the door vs discovered later
- Repeat orders per shop per month (target 4+)
- Records shared with a funder or supplier (target 50)

--- UNIQUE VALUE PROPOSITION ----------------------------------------------

"Reorder from the suppliers you already trust, pay safely, and build
proof your business is real, without extra paperwork."
Not a marketplace: Akayza strengthens the relationships traders
already have, and every payment becomes evidence both sides agree on.

--- UNFAIR ADVANTAGE ------------------------------------------------------

- Cash confirmed by BOTH parties, sealed and tamper-evident, even
  offline: no competitor proves a cash handover between two sides.
- Supplier-led growth: one wholesaler brings its existing ~50 shops.
- The record belongs to the trader and is post-quantum ready (ML-DSA),
  so it stays trustworthy for years.
- Built by a team from the community, with a working platform
  foundation already in place.

--- SECURITY REQUIREMENTS (RISK ASSESSMENT) -------------------------------

Assets: trader identity and login; credit-book customer names and
amounts; order and payment records; the sealed trading record;
delivery addresses; supplier catalogue and prices; signing keys.

Top risks (likelihood x impact):
1. Account takeover (weak password, stolen phone): HIGH
2. Tampering with payment history to fake a record: HIGH
3. One side claiming a different cash amount: HIGH
4. Fake payment confirmations (spoofed PayFast notification): HIGH
5. Reading another shop's books or addresses (broken access control):
   HIGH
6. Leak of personal data (POPIA): MEDIUM-HIGH
7. Shared-record links forwarded or guessed: MEDIUM

Requirements: every route role-pinned and owner-checked; passwords
hashed (bcrypt/argon2), email verified before use; tokens short-lived
and stored in Android Keystore; money never held by Akayza; payments
marked PAID only by a verified PayFast notification; records
append-only, hash-chained and signed; POPIA: minimal data, owner
controls sharing, deletion closes all links.

--- THREAT MODELLING & DESIGN REVIEW --------------------------------------

STRIDE on the order-to-payment flow:
- Spoofing: fake PayFast callback -> verify signature, source host,
  amount against our DB, and post-back validation; idempotent.
- Tampering: editing an old payment (R2,340 -> R23,400) -> SHA-256 hash
  chain + Ed25519 signatures; any change breaks the chain (live demo).
  Server seals also carry ML-DSA (post-quantum).
- Repudiation: "I paid a different amount" -> both parties type their
  amount independently; a mismatch becomes a DISPUTE on both records.
- Information disclosure: object-level checks on every read; shared
  records show aggregates only, never customer names or addresses;
  links expire and are revocable, and every view is logged.
- Denial of service: rate limits on login, codes, uploads and public
  pages; batch sizes capped.
- Elevation of privilege: roles fixed at account creation; writable
  fields whitelisted; the app can only create trader accounts.
Design review: the public record page and the PayFast receiver run as
separate processes; the main API is never exposed publicly.

--- DEVELOPMENT (SECURE CODING PRACTICES) ---------------------------------

- OWASP API Security Top 10 as the checklist.
- Input validated on the server (schemas); the phone's prices are
  never trusted, since orders are re-priced from the database.
- Parameterised queries (SQLAlchemy ORM); no string-built SQL.
- Secrets only in environment files, never committed; separate keys
  per service (a Akayza-only Cloudinary key), rotated after the event.
- Passwords hashed; verification codes and reset tokens stored hashed,
  single-use, short expiry.
- Private signing keys never leave the phone (Android Keystore).
- Uploads: images only, size-capped, signed uploads; supplier image
  links fetched with SSRF protection (https only, no private IPs).
- Canonical JSON rules so hashes match across TypeScript and Python.
- Code review on every change; AI assistants used for routine screens
  only, never for crypto or payment code, and reviewed.

--- SECURITY TESTING --------------------------------------------------------

- Shared test vector: the same hash and signature in TypeScript and
  Python.
- Access-control tests with a second account (can't read another
  shop's books, orders or addresses).
- Tamper test: edit a sealed payment in the database -> chain break
  detected and shown.
- PayFast tests: forged notification, wrong amount, replayed
  notification -> all rejected.
- Auth tests: brute-force rate limits, expired and reused codes, reset
  link reuse.
- Every route has a declared role (automated test walks all routes).
- Dependency audit (npm audit / pip-audit) before the demo build.

--- ASSESSMENT & SECURE INTEGRATION ---------------------------------------

- PayFast: sandbox for the demo; server-side signature, IP and amount
  checks; Akayza never touches card data (PayFast's hosted page).
- Google Sign-In: ID tokens verified on the server (audience, issuer,
  expiry, verified email); existing accounts linked only after the
  password is proven.
- Cloudinary: signed uploads only, own API key, akayza/ folder.
- Mapbox and email: tokens kept server-side; free tiers with usage
  alerts.
- POPIA: purpose-limited data; customer names stay private to the
  trader; addresses visible only to that order's supplier; account
  deletion closes shared links; a privacy notice in the app.
- Honest limits: "confirmed by both" proves two people stated the
  same amount, not that the cash was real; two colluding parties can
  agree on a lie, so patterns over time are flagged.
- Before real users: a real SMS/email domain setup, secrets rotated,
  a penetration test, a legal POPIA review, and a security.txt contact.
