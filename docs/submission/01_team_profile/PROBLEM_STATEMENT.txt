---------------------------------------------------------------------------
PASTE-READY (about 200 words)
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

South Africa's informal traders -- spaza shops, informal builders,
street vendors and resellers -- run their business on phone calls,
WhatsApp messages, cash and a notebook. A spaza owner restocks from a
wholesaler and pays a driver in cash; a builder buys materials and gets
paid by a client in stages; none of it leaves a record that both sides
accept. So suppliers can't reconcile cash, builders and clients argue
over what was paid, and the trader can't prove the business is real.

The scale is large. The township economy is worth about R900 billion a
year, R180 billion of it through spaza shops, yet about 80% of township
businesses are unregistered, fewer than 9% can get a bank loan and 57%
survive on personal savings (Standard Bank, 2025). Informal work is
about a third of all jobs in South Africa, and Stats SA now defines an
informal business partly by whether it keeps records at all. When
government opened a R500 million Spaza Shop Support Fund, only R179.6
million had reached 2,369 shops by May 2026: 354 applications failed
verification (non-existent businesses, ownership mismatches) and only
58% of applicants held a valid licence (DSBD, 2026). Real traders can't
prove themselves, and fake ones slip in.


---------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE FIVE PARTS (what the organisers asked for)
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. WHO
   Informal traders in South African townships:
   - spaza shop owners (the largest group: trade is 48.2% of informal
     businesses; Stats SA);
   - informal builders and trades (plumbers, electricians, painters):
     construction is one of the informal industries that is growing;
   - other hustles: street vendors, resellers, food sellers.
   And the people on the other side of every deal: the wholesalers and
   hardware suppliers they buy from, and the clients who pay builders.

2. WHAT
   Business runs on calls, WhatsApp, cash and a notebook, with no record
   both sides accept. So:
   (a) orders, cash payments and stage payments can't be confirmed by
       both sides, which leads to disputes (a driver's cash that doesn't
       add up, a client who says a stage was already paid);
   (b) nothing proves the business trades, so it can't reach credit,
       support funds or better supplier terms;
   (c) suppliers can't reconcile what drivers collect or what's owed.

3. WHERE
   South African townships: at the spaza counter, on building sites, on
   the phone and WhatsApp, and at the door when stock, work and cash
   change hands. We start in Gauteng townships (Tembisa, Ivory Park) and
   Limpopo, where our team is.

4. WHY IT MATTERS AT SCALE
   - R900 billion township economy; R180 billion through spaza shops.
   - About a third of South Africa's jobs are informal (half for people
     aged 15-24).
   - About 80% of township businesses are unregistered; fewer than 9%
     can get a bank loan; 57% are self-funded.
   - Even funded help can't reach them: R179.6 million of a R500 million
     fund deployed, because verification fails in both directions.
   - Late payment and non-payment is a chronic problem even in formal
     construction; informal builders have no contracts to fall back on.

5. EVIDENCE
   - Standard Bank, Township Informal Economy Report 2025: R900bn
     economy; spaza about R180bn; ~80% unregistered; <9% bank loans;
     57% self-funded; 49% operate from home/garage; >56% prefer EFT
     (they are ready for digital payment).
   - Stats SA: trade is 48.2% of informal businesses (2023, down from
     ~70% in 2001) as construction, transport and services grow;
     informal jobs are about a third of all employment; from Q3 2025 an
     informal business is defined partly by whether it keeps records for
     tax purposes.
   - DSBD / dtic, May 2026: R179.6m approved for 2,369 shops from the
     R500m Spaza Shop Support Fund; 354 applications failed verification
     (non-existent businesses, ownership discrepancies, applicant vs
     operator mismatches); only 58% linked to a valid licence or permit.
   - Construction payment studies (formal sector, SA): 89% of surveyed
     practitioners agree (63%) or strongly agree (26%) that late and
     non-payment is chronic.
