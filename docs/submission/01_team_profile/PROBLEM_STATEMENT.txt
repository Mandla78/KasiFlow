PASTE-READY (about 170 words)
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

Spaza shop owners in South Africa's townships restock from wholesalers
through phone calls, WhatsApp messages and cash handed to delivery
drivers, and none of it leaves a record that both sides accept. That
missing record costs them twice: a supplier can't reconcile the cash,
and the owner can't prove the business trades at all.

The scale is large. Spaza shops turn over about R180 billion a year
inside a R900 billion township economy, yet about 80% of township
businesses are unregistered, fewer than 9% can get a bank loan and 57%
survive on personal savings (Standard Bank, 2025). When government
opened a R500 million Spaza Shop Support Fund, only R179.6 million had
reached 2,369 shops by May 2026: 354 applications failed verification
(non-existent businesses, ownership mismatches) and only 58% of
applicants held a valid municipal licence (DSBD, May 2026). Real shops
can't prove themselves, and fake ones slip in. The owners are ready to
change: more than 56% already prefer EFT to cash (Standard Bank, 2025).


---------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE FIVE PARTS (what the organisers asked for)
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. WHO
   Spaza shop owners in South African townships: small, often
   home-based (49% trade from a home or garage), mostly unregistered,
   self-funded. And the wholesalers who supply them, who carry the
   other half of every unrecorded order and cash handover.

2. WHAT
   Restocking runs on calls, WhatsApp and cash with no shared record.
   So: (a) orders and cash payments can't be confirmed by both sides,
   (b) nothing proves the shop trades, so it can't reach credit,
   support funds or better supplier terms, and (c) wholesalers can't
   reconcile what drivers collect.

3. WHERE
   South African townships, in the trade between spaza shops and their
   wholesalers: at the counter, on the phone, and at the door when
   stock and cash change hands. We start in Gauteng townships
   (Tembisa, Ivory Park) and Limpopo, where our team is.

4. WHY IT MATTERS AT SCALE
   R180 billion a year flows through spaza shops (R900 billion township
   economy). About 80% of township businesses are unregistered and
   fewer than 9% can get a bank loan. Even a funded government
   programme couldn't get money to them: R179.6 million of R500 million
   deployed, because verification fails in both directions.

5. EVIDENCE
   - Standard Bank, Township Informal Economy Report 2025: R900bn
     economy; spaza about R180bn; ~80% unregistered; <9% bank loans;
     57% self-funded; 49% operate from home/garage; >56% prefer EFT.
   - DSBD / dtic update, May 2026: R179.6m approved for 2,369 shops
     from the R500m fund; 354 applications failed verification
     (non-existent businesses, ownership discrepancies, applicant vs
     operator mismatches); only 58% linked to a valid licence/permit.
